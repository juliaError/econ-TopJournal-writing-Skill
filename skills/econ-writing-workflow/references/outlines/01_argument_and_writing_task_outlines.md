# Argument Outlines And Writing-Task Outlines

Use for planning or revising a paper/book or proposal outline, a writing-task
outline that human collaborators must use to write sections, or an associated
research-team staffing brief. Reuse the existing
argument-logic, literature-grounding, and table/figure modules as needed;
this reference supplies the handoff form and division of work.

## Audience And Recipient Necessity

Apply the main skill's Audience And Intended Use step before drafting. Record
an established audience and purpose in existing working context when needed
for resumption, not as mandatory front matter in the delivered document.
Reapply the check when a self-use draft becomes a document for others.

Judge content by what this recipient needs to understand, decide or do:

| Intended use | Useful content | Content normally left out |
|---|---|---|
| Author's own thinking | Research reasoning, useful alternatives, unresolved questions and relevant edit notes | Unnecessary bookkeeping or explanations the author did not request |
| Discussion with a teacher or collaborator | Research question, reasoning, evidence, feasibility and concrete choices on which input is sought | Author-agent editing dialogue, rejected drafting history, personal availability/workload constraints and personal accommodation arrangements |
| Execution by section writers | Confirmed assignments, usable sources, argument steps, figure/table specifications and necessary handoffs | Personal availability/workload constraints, allocation negotiation history, personal accommodation arrangements and redundant coordination commentary |

Use the deletion test: would removing this passage make the recipient less
able to understand the research, make the requested decision or complete the
assigned work? If not, leave it outside the circulated document. If yes,
state the necessary current information directly, without recounting the
internal discussion. Research justifications, evidence qualifications and
proposal methods or risks must not be mistaken for editing history. Do not
silently remove accepted claims, sources or assignments under an audience
cleanup; seek a specific decision when their substantive removal is needed.

For a staffing brief intended for execution, give only confirmed owners and
scope, plus necessary outputs or handoffs. When the user explicitly requests
a proposed allocation for discussion, mark proposed owners and responsibilities
as pending confirmation; do not treat the proposal as a formal assignment or
execution authorization. Do not duplicate the full writing-task outline.
Keep personal availability, workload, existing task burdens and personal
accommodation arrangements only in the user's private deliberation or records.
Do not include them in documents handed to anyone else, including teachers,
colleagues or collaborators, whether for discussion or execution. The
recipient-necessity test does not create an exception. Retain relevant expertise
only when the user permits its disclosure and it is necessary for the
recipient's current decision; do not recount negotiation history.
Shared case/material coordination belongs in the brief only when it is part
of the confirmed scope and recipients need it to carry out a concrete task
or handoff. Do not turn the agent's ideas for consistency, workload balancing
or avoiding repetition into additional assignments. Do not restore a deleted
coordination block merely because it sounds useful in general.

For example, “甲负责2.1；乙负责4.2” states an assignment. “考虑到甲临近毕业，
我们几轮讨论后作此调整” explains an internal decision. “甲提供已确认的共用
数据表，乙据此撰写比较” may be useful when that handoff was requested and is
necessary. Do not invent it simply because the same data appear twice.

## 1. Choose The Deliverable And Preserve The Accepted Plan

- A discussion outline organizes questions, claims, alternatives, and section
  roles. It need not specify every writing or production task.
- A research proposal explains research to be undertaken. Use
  `../research-convergence/03_proposal_revision_stop_gates.md` for evidence
  status, design, feasibility, stages, and risks. A task outline can accompany
  a proposal but does not replace those requirements.
- A writing-task outline tells a writer what to read, explain, and produce.
  Writing instructions and figure specifications are intended content here;
  do not remove them using manuscript-only voice rules.
- A staffing brief states confirmed owners, work and necessary handoffs for
  execution, or clearly marked pending proposals when the user requests a
  discussion version; it is not a second writing-task outline or a history
  of allocation.
- Manuscript prose presents the argument to the eventual reader. Keep task
  instructions out of that separate deliverable.

Use the author's latest accepted framework and named working file. Preserve
accepted detail when converting a discussion into assignments. A source
ledger or overview table can support the outline but cannot replace writing
the accepted tasks into it. Do not create competing outlines or silently
rename, reorder, or replace accepted headings when only detail edits were
requested; identify any proposed heading changes explicitly.

Let the central question organize chapters and sections. Literature supplies
relevant reasoning and evidence; its table of contents should not displace an
accepted structure. A mechanism that operates across chapters need not become
a separate chapter. If conceptual synthesis is requested, define each proposed
concept, its economic relationship, and its additional explanatory value;
a new label alone does not establish an original theory.

Before expanding detailed writing tasks, map the argument:
central question -> chapter/section questions -> claims -> reasoning steps
and paragraph purposes. Use the accepted outline, not a newly invented
framework. Each paragraph should have an identifiable job in answering its
parent question; it need not repeat the book's central question.

Use a compact argument diagram and allocation table, for example:

| Claim and importance | Parent question | Main body location | Other necessary locations and their jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core claim or supporting point | What it helps answer | Where its full reasoning and evidence belong | Where it needs introduction or synthesis; leave blank if unnecessary |

Give each independent claim one main location for its full explanation.
Core claims may also need introduction and synthesis at several levels;
supporting points often need only their body location. Choose appearances by
purpose, never by a fixed five- or seven-location quota. This map is a
navigation and responsibility view of the accepted outline, not a competing
plan or a substitute for the assignments.

For paper/book outline projects, proactively place the compact current
argument diagram and allocation table in the project's standard `AGENTS.md`,
with a link to the authoritative outline and an explicit maintenance rule.
Include the map itself, not only a promise to make it or a link to another
map. Do not create a parallel `Agent.md`. Honor persistent-file authorization:
use applicable explicit authorization, including authorized ongoing map
maintenance; if it is absent, prepare the exact block and request permission
for that file. Skill invocation or manuscript-edit permission alone is not
permission to edit `AGENTS.md`.

The maintenance rule in `AGENTS.md` must assign responsibility to the agent:
whenever the author confirms a change to the core question, subquestions,
claims, main locations, or chapter/section roles, update the map and its
source references in the same work round under the applicable authorization.
Do not wait for the author to remind the agent. On resumption, check the map
against the current accepted outline. Keep it current-state only; proposals
and rejected alternatives must not silently replace accepted content.
The authoritative plan and outline continue to govern substance. Keep the
map concise and leave detailed assignments and evidence in the outline.
Do not duplicate task-record content in the map: leave task revisions, marker
states, execution progress, and acceptance criteria in their owning task or
checkpoint records. If the map differs from the current accepted outline,
reconcile it from that outline under the applicable write authorization.

For Chinese headings, use clear language that identifies the economic object,
relationship or advantage. For Chinese policy and development topics, prefer
established official wording when its meaning fits; for technical concepts,
use intelligible disciplinary terms. Verify the source before calling a
phrase an official formulation. Otherwise use direct, concrete Chinese
rather than a coined compound. Explain what an ambiguous term refers to
before choosing a label; do not replace it with a familiar but substantively
different concept. Original concepts require a clear definition and
explanatory contribution, not merely a novel name.

## 2. Make Each Assignment Executable

Use the smallest set of tasks sufficient for the section's purpose. State
what the writer must explain, with which material, and in what logical order.
Do not turn every reasoning step or example into a formal subsection.

For a Chinese writing-task outline, a useful default is:

```text
2.1.1 [subsection title; generated by the document's numbering system]
资料：Author/Institution (year/date), relevant section/table/passage and its use.
要点：
任务1：[the question or argument to develop]
（1）[define the object and state the first explanation]
（2）[explain how the first step leads to the next]
任务2：[a task requiring both a figure/table and interpretation]
图表制作：[the specified output, inputs, and arrangement]
正文写作：[the comparison to explain and its role in the argument]
```

Adapt labels to the author's preferred format. Put sources before the related
tasks; with several unrelated sources, attach each to its task instead of
leaving a long undifferentiated reading list. Necessary presentation
instructions can sit inside the relevant task rather than being repeated in
a separate end-of-section field. A task may contain both production and
writing, but distinguish those actions visibly.

Replace vague instructions such as "observe", "compare", "connect back",
or "explain the relationship" with named objects and outputs. For example,
"制作一张各国人口与消费规模表；正文解释人口与平均购买量怎样共同形成总量"
is more executable than "观察规模并比较各经济体". Specify which countries
and why they provide useful comparisons when those choices are settled.

Avoid adding completion rubrics, tracking IDs, or workflow commentary to
every task when its positive instructions already define the required work.
Keep internal edit history such as "restore the previously arranged table"
out of the delivered outline; say what table to make or use.

## 3. Specify Facts And Figures Without Inventing More Work

Give each fact a role: shared background, the phenomenon to explain, a case
process, or evidence for a particular argument. In a book introduction,
prefer a small set of overall stylized facts (典型事实) that directly establish
the central question; do not add generic background for nominal balance.
Use a brief detailed example there only when it has a clear problem-framing,
narrative or reader-attention function. Put its full process and other
detailed facts where the relevant mechanisms are developed. Do not
concentrate all data in the introduction or remove a necessary side of the
background merely to shorten it.
Do not replace aggregate scale, composition, or coverage facts with isolated
cases; use cases to explain the processes behind the facts.

For each required display, make the following recoverable:

- how many tables/figures are needed and what each is for;
- whether to use an existing source display, redraw it, or construct a new one;
- table rows and columns, or chart type, axes, series/panels and grouping;
- input source, variables, objects, period and necessary statistical scope;
- the comparison or pattern the accompanying prose must explain.

For maps and distributions, identify the geographic unit or grouping rule
when settled. Preserve the approved design and route unresolved substantive
data choices to the existing empirical clarification rules; do not invent
countries, years, samples, or imputation just to fill the instructions.

Separate statistical definitions from display units. Preserve needed
comparability, such as price basis or population coverage, but allow readable
display magnitudes and unit conversions with clear labels. Do not fix a
particular display unit or base year unnecessarily.

When new construction is not explicitly required, prefer a sufficient
published aggregate to unnecessary new data production. A study covering
186 experiments can support a summary row; its
coverage does not imply an assignment to describe all 186 cases. Within the
authorized editing scope, keep a settled execution outline focused on required
displays; do not add nonessential optional displays or silently remove an
accepted output. Keep genuinely unresolved alternatives in the discussion
or proposal stage, with their status clear.

## 4. Keep Sources Usable And Proportionate

Use a short author/institution name plus year/date beside the task; put full
titles, versions, links and bibliographic details in one reference list.
Specify the useful source location and its role at the level actually needed:
chapter, section, page, original table/figure, or distinctive words where
versions differ. Do not require long opening/closing quotations for every
reference or repeat full bibliographic entries at each summary.

Writers must be able to identify and obtain the material. Internal reading
codes, extraction-line numbers and private folder names are insufficient
handoff references. A locally saved published or publicly downloadable paper
remains a valid source; the issue is recoverability, not where this copy lives.
Do not invent a public source identity or claim access that was not verified.

Select material for a specific explanatory use. When a source list grows,
first check whether the task has accumulated unnecessary auxiliary questions.
Remove unused references and duplicate explanations before simply shortening
citations. Source ledgers remain useful supporting records, not substitutes
for sources and instructions beside the writing task.

## 5. Explain The Argument And The Use Of Cases

Assign every reasoning step and paragraph a purpose: what it establishes,
why that step is needed, and how it advances the local argument. This is a
planning and review requirement, not a requirement to print a purpose label
in manuscript prose or impose a fixed paragraph count.

For an A-to-B-to-C argument, define the objects and explain both transitions.
An ordered list of nouns is not a mechanism. For instance, demand aggregation
may support specialized investment because a larger recurring volume spreads
an indivisible setup cost; that capacity can then support sustained supply.
Name the relevant actors, choices and necessary conditions instead of merely
repeating the arrows.

When using a classic paper, state the principle taken from it and how the
current work applies or extends that principle to its central question.
Distinguish the cited result from the author's additional reasoning.
Do not require a separate published paper for every author-developed idea.
An explicit conceptual explanation or hypothesis can be planned without an
existing causal estimate; it cannot be presented as an observed fact or
attributed to a source that does not establish it. Paraphrasing still requires
appropriate attribution of borrowed ideas.

Use each case for a named explanatory job. When two cases illustrate the same
mechanism, group them and state their common use. When they show different
steps or conditions, state what the later case adds. Distinguish background
facts, the case process, the mechanism, its conditions, and the chapter's
broader claim rather than mixing them in one long instruction.

The same case can recur if each use answers a different question. Do not
retell its entire history or replace it with a new case merely to avoid a
repeated name. Apply the same test to reasoning and facts: another appearance
needs a distinct introductory, explanatory or synthesizing job, not another
full exposition. Prefer inspected real processes for central explanations;
identify hypothetical illustrations as such. Where several tasks use stages
of one process, prefer one coherent process figure over repeated near-identical
diagrams unless separate views do distinct work.

## 6. Give Introductions And Conclusions Distinct Jobs

For a book outline, use these responsibilities as adaptable defaults:

| Location | Primary job |
|---|---|
| General introduction | Establish the shared question through limited relevant overall facts, state the main judgment and necessary concepts, and explain the route through the book. |
| Chapter/section opening | Introduce the local question, its role in the wider argument, and a brief claim or route; use a fact or contrast only if useful, without prewriting the full explanation. |
| Body subsection | Develop the needed reasoning, facts, cases and evidence at their main location. |
| Section/chapter ending | Summarize what this part established at its own level and explain its connection to adjacent parts. |
| General conclusion | Answer the central question by synthesizing established chapter conclusions and relationships, including conditions already examined in the body. |

At every level, a summary must not introduce a new claim, example, evidence
item or argument requiring fresh support. It may connect established
conclusions and explain their significance at the appropriate level. If a
concluding statement needs new evidence or a new reasoning sequence, move
that work to the body. Do not turn the general conclusion into successive
chapter abstracts or a second literature review.

When the introduction summarizes body mechanisms, draft that summary after
the relevant body is settled or explicitly assign it as a later synthesis.
Move missing substantive explanations into their body locations rather than
expanding a conclusion to carry them. Do not force a fact or case into every
opening; a conclusion may appropriately begin by recalling the argument.
For a mechanism-focused section, stating the central point before the cases
often clarifies their purpose; do not enforce a fixed three-paragraph pattern.

## 7. Design For Concision And Limit Cross-Section Dependence

Prevent repetition before drafting detailed assignments. Allocate full
background, mechanism explanations and case processes to their main body
locations; elsewhere explicitly assign only the necessary introduction,
recap or synthesis. Do not routinely draft every explanation several times
and rely on a final compression pass. Add sources and technical work only
for the argument the task actually needs to establish.

Before merging similar tasks, identify each one's additional question. Merge
when that difference is negligible; retain and clarify when the same process
is examined at genuinely different stages or levels. For example, collaboration
between organizations, engineering tasks, and learning during use need not
be the same assignment even when they share a product example.

Reduce repeated setup, source descriptions, auxiliary branches and repeated
mechanisms before removing necessary facts or reasoning links. Scale technical
detail to the genre: a synthetic book may use a paper's economic reasoning
without assigning its identification strategy or model derivation; research
proposals and empirical/theoretical papers still need the methods essential
to their own claims. Do not impose a universal section cap, source quota,
compression percentage, preferred author, or fixed example set.

Make each assignment's material, required work and new content understandable
locally. This does not require reproducing another section's full reasoning.
Cite the appropriate source rather than sending the writer through a chain
of other sections. Where useful, one sentence can say "前节说明……，
本节进一步说明……". Keep cross-references that genuinely supply shared
definitions, indispensable arguments, data or an already-produced display;
do not pursue zero cross-references by duplicating that work.

## 8. Review Actual Tasks And Maintain Real Document Structure

Read each unit within the requested audit scope, with its nearby context.
Check whether the writer can identify the question, necessary material,
argument sequence, required outputs, and what this unit adds. If the user
points to an example of a general problem, inspect the stated broader scope,
not only the example. State the scope actually reviewed.

Before handing off the document, read each paragraph, heading and task within
the delivery scope from the established recipient's perspective. Apply the
recipient-necessity test above, check for unconfirmed extra assignments, and
verify that cleanup preserved necessary sources, reasoning and production
instructions. A self-use draft need not be stripped of useful internal notes.
Keep the audit and internal discussion in existing records or the response;
do not append them to the handoff document or create another version by default.
Keyword scans may help locate passages but cannot establish this review.

Check the argument map against the tasks: each claim has a clear parent
question and main location; reasoning steps have a necessary purpose; other
appearances perform their assigned jobs; summaries use only established
claims and relationships. Check that the map in `AGENTS.md` reflects accepted
changes and that its agent-owned maintenance rule is present.

Scripts can extract units, show them in sequence, check numbering, or track
coverage. They may display the argument allocation and flag possible repeated
full explanations for the agent to read and assess directly. They must not automatically
copy a claim into every opening and ending, substitute for reading the
argument, or establish a semantic pass from keyword matches, complete fields,
or command success.
Use lightweight notes as needed; do not automatically impose the complete-paper
multiagent/hash protocol on an outline. Existing authorization and evidence
requirements still apply to the actual work undertaken.

For LaTeX, use the document class's numbered hierarchy instead of typing
formal numbers into bold text. A chapter-based book/report normally uses
chapter/section/subsection; an article uses section/subsection/subsubsection.
Mark introductions and summaries as auxiliary headings where appropriate,
with a consistent appearance and explicit role. Let counters, labels and
references manage numbering and dependencies. Respect the authorized class,
hierarchy and layout scope when implementing a conversion.

Check content clarity separately from compilation, numbering, contents and
rendered layout. A successful compile does not prove the assignment is clear.
When only figure/table tasks are specified, review their specifications and
source feasibility; inspect rendered labels and visual output when the actual
figures/tables are produced. A proposal skeleton similarly receives an outline
review, with complete-proposal acceptance reserved for a complete proposal.
Make accepted corrections in the designated outline and preserve user edits;
do not report a task-record update as a manuscript or outline update.

## Writing Guidance Behind These Rules

- John H. Cochrane, *Writing Tips for Ph. D. Students* (2005), pp. 1-4:
  identify the central contribution, remove unnecessary preliminaries and
  repetition, and keep conclusions brief.
  https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/phd_paper_writing.pdf
- Harvard College Writing Center, *Introductions*: give enough context to
  understand the thesis; reserve unnecessary detail for the body.
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/introductions
- Harvard College Writing Center, *Anatomy of a Body Paragraph*: give each
  paragraph a main point and explain how its evidence supports the argument.
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/anatomy-body-paragraph
- Harvard College Writing Center, *Conclusions*: synthesize significance
  instead of completely restating the paper.
  https://writingcenter.fas.harvard.edu/conclusions
- University of Wisconsin-Madison Writing Center, *Creating a Reverse
  Outline*: examine each paragraph's subject and function to diagnose
  sequence, missing links and repetition.
  https://writing.wisc.edu/handbook/processandstructure/reverseoutlines/

The forward argument map is this workflow's adaptation of those structural
checks, not a prescribed five-location method from these sources. The
no-new-argument summary rule follows the author's requested convention; the
guides allow disciplinary variation and do not all impose that exact rule.
